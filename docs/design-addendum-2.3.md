# Oar and Ember: Design Addendum 2.3

Date: 11 November 2026. Approved by the user. Builds on Addendum 2.2 and wins where they
conflict. Integrate it into the books named in each section, then treat the books as the source
of truth. Items marked PROPOSAL go in unless the user vetoes them at the next review.

---

## 1. The cruelty of every tradition (Books 1, 4, 5, 6, 8)

Every religious tradition in the game is shown with its cruelty as well as its comfort, even-
handedly, through what its believers do. No tradition is the good one.

| Tradition | Cruelty shown in play |
|---|---|
| Norse belief | Thralls killed to follow a chieftain into death; Heid giving captured crews to Rán; blood-oaths that bind sons to their fathers' feuds; Orm drowning hostages whose families failed to pay |
| The Church at Kilbrecc | Bondmen who work the tithe farms and cannot leave; public floggings for penance; the abbot's curse (a fast and a malediction against a household, the Gaelic custom of fasting against an enemy); hired swords when prayer fails; shrines of the older faith smashed |
| Gaelic custom | Satire that can ruin a person; the blood-price that lets the rich kill the poor; hostages starved when treaties break; geasa that doom people to impossible choices |
| The Keepers and the old powers | Seal-kin women bound by whoever holds their skin; the sick left on a skerry for the sea to judge; the Bog King's drowned offerings in the peat |

Rules: shown, not glorified; always with believers who are kind, doubtful or ashamed too. Hard
limits remain: no children as actors, no sexual violence, no player torture.

## 2. Ordinary people, and who gets depth (Books 5, 8)

- **Most people are ordinary**, with simple motives: food, safety, family, money, pride, fear,
  a grudge, a debt. Avoid making minor characters schemers or philosophers.
- **Minor characters** (farmers, traders, guards, travellers) have one or two lines, drawn from
  pools that vary by culture, season, time of day, weather and what the household has done.
- **Major characters**, meaning leaders, antagonists, residents and quest characters, have
  **branching conversation and reaction trees** that change with:
  - time of day and season (a different greeting at dawn, at a winter fire, on a feast day);
  - the household's deeds and reputation;
  - their happiness, friendship and fear towards the steerer;
  - quest and story state;
  - recent events (a death, a raid, a storm).
- **Minimum depth for each major character:** greetings for each season and time of day;
  at least three topic trees; reactions to at least ten deed types; callbacks to remembered
  events; a change in voice when friendship or fear crosses a threshold.

## 3. Travel between sites, and points of interest (Books 1, 2, 4, 9, 10; replaces the walk-between-areas model in 2.2 section 3)

- **Sites are explored on foot; travel between them is not.** From a map, the player chooses a
  destination and the journey happens off screen, costing game time. On arrival, the site is
  explored in real time.
- **A day fits one or two sites.** With about 17 real minutes per day (06:00 to 02:00), a trip
  of about two game hours each way plus exploration leaves room for one or two sites in a day.
- **Starting travel times** (game hours from Carrick Strand; similar from Saltness):

| Destination | Time | Destination | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carrick Landing | 0.5 h | Móin Mhór | 4 h |
| Ardcarn | 1 h | Drowned Trackway | 5 h |
| Kilbrecc and its farms | 2 h | Sliabh Carn | 5 h |
| Loch Carn | 2 h | Cailleach's Cave | 6 h |
| Black Wood edge | 3 h | High Pass | 6 h |
| Black Wood heart | 4 h | Dún Fiach (Ridge Road) | 4 h |
| River Ford | 4 h | Salt pans and Saltvik | 5 h |
| Mound Field and moor | 6 h | Saltness | 5.5 h |

- **Modifiers:** night travel x1.5 and riskier; driving cattle or carrying cargo x1.5; known roads
  x0.8; the kelpie bridle halves water crossings; blocked routes (landslide, spate, snow, flood)
  force detours or are closed, as in 2.2.
- **Journey events:** each leg can be interrupted by a short playable scene: travellers, bandits,
  wolves, weather, a wounded stranger, or a discovery ("smoke in the hills" adds a new point of
  interest). Most journeys are quiet.
- **Camping:** if night falls away from home, camp at the site or on the road (as in 2.2).

### Points of interest in every site
- **Major points of interest (1 or 2 per site):** a village, dungeon, ruin, shrine or camp with
  people and quests.
- **Minor points of interest (4 to 8 per site):** a cairn, a wreck, a carved stone, a hermit's
  hut, a hidden cache, a viewpoint, an eagle's nest, a herb patch, a grave, a burned farm.
- **Off the beaten track:** the best minor rewards lie off the obvious paths, behind a climb, a
  wade, a hazard or a puzzle, and reward curiosity with food, materials, lore, collectibles or
  equipment.
- **Hazards** (as in 2.2) guard many of the best finds.

## 4. Story gates instead of fixed dates (Books 1, 3, 4, 10)

- **The story moves forward only through major events the player chooses to start**: a raid,
  an expedition, a defence, a council. These are **gates**.
- **The calendar keeps running.** Seasons turn, crops grow and winter must be prepared for, but
  no chapter deadline passes without the player: dated story events wait at their gate.
  Pressure builds gently while the player delays (demands grow, rumours spread), but never
  forces a gate.
- **Each gate has a readiness screen** (crew, ship, stores, allies, equipment) and a clear warning
  that it is a point of no return.
- **Gates (PROPOSAL):** the launch (end of Chapter 1); the Carrick raid; the reply to Asgrim; the
  defence of Skarvholm; the household council; the move ashore; the Gathering; the Return to
  Skarvholm; the final battle.
- Chapter windows in Book 4 become **typical** dates rather than limits. The Return's "one retry
  before day 255" becomes "one retry before you set out for the final battle".
- Book 10's ideal progression stays as a guide for a well-paced player, not a deadline.

## 5. People like Dwarf Fortress: traits, likes, relationships, tendencies (Books 2, 5, 8, 10)

Every resident and companion has:
- **Traits (two to four)**, some visible at once and some discovered over time. Starting set:

| Trait | Effect |
|---|---|
| Berserk | In combat, when wounded or when a friend falls, may go berserk: ignores orders, attacks the nearest enemy wildly (including those surrendering, and civilians in reach), deals more damage, defends less. Deeds count against the household |
| Cowardly | Breaks first when cohesion falls; may flee a fight; hates night raids |
| Superstitious | Refuses tasks or voyages after bad omens; panics at supernatural signs; insists on rites |
| Brave | Holds the line longer; volunteers for danger |
| Greedy | Pockets loot; argues over shares |
| Pious (to a given faith) | Prays daily; reacts strongly to sacrilege and to its own faith's cruelty |
| Hot-tempered | Starts disputes; quick to fight |
| Gentle | Calms disputes; hates killing |
| Drinker | Drinks at feasts and in hard times; unreliable the morning after |
| Light-fingered | Steals small things from the stores (Oddi) |
| Keen-eyed | Spots things first at sea and on watch |
| Sea-sick | Worse at sea; better on land |
| Storyteller or singer | Raises happiness at the fire |
| Grudge-holder | Remembers every slight; feuds |
| Diligent or lazy | Works more or less without supervision |

- **Likes and dislikes:** foods (salt fish, honey, ale, kale), activities (fishing, carving,
  fighting), places, weather, people and gifts. Meeting them raises happiness; ignoring them
  does not hurt, but dislikes do.
- **Relationships between residents** form and break through shared events: friends, rivals,
  grudges, mentors, drinking companions, family. No romance system; existing marriages and
  family ties remain.
- **Tendencies:** small daily behaviours that make people readable: one prays at dawn, one
  wanders the shore at night, one hoards stones, one sings at the oars.
- Traits affect work, combat behaviour, reactions to deeds, disputes and conversation.
- The household screen shows each person's known traits, likes, relationships, happiness,
  friendship and fear, with a line on why each is what it is.

## 6. Optional quests for equipment and requests; companions (Books 1, 4, 6)

- **Optional quests** can be mounted at any time for: ship enhancements (a better sail, a carved
  prow, a sea-charm, a famous mast timber), character equipment (the dungeon rewards in 2.2,
  plus smaller finds), and residents' requests (2.2 section 9).
- **Companions:** the steerer can take up to two friendly characters on any expedition on foot.
  They fight, help with puzzles and hazards, comment, react through their traits and can be
  wounded. Some quests need a particular companion (for example, Runa to judge a mast timber, or
  Senán to read a carved stone).

## 7. Downed, not dead, and scripted deaths (Books 1, 2, 5, 6, 10)

- **Downed friendly characters generally survive** if the steerer brings them back to the boat
  or the village before the scene ends. Downed allies do not bleed out on a timer; carrying them
  is slow and leaves the carrier unable to fight. Enemies do not finish off the downed, except
  berserkers and a few named cruel enemies, and those do so with a visible warning.
- **Left behind** at the end of a scene, a downed ally is captured or killed, according to the
  enemy (captives can be rescued or ransomed later).
- Crew permadeath now comes mainly from being left behind, from berserkers and cruel enemies, and
  from **scripted deaths**. The *Rán's mercy* option still prevents unscripted deaths.
- **Scripted deaths and death-choices (PROPOSAL):**
  - **Rán's debt:** Thorkel's bargain (2.2 twist 1) comes due: someone must go into the sea.
    Gudrun offers herself; the steerer can accept, choose otherwise, or refuse and pay another way
    (the ship, the household's luck or a later life).
  - **The Return:** someone must hold the cairn door as the tide rises. Whoever stays dies unless
    the party found the spring-room passage first.
  - **Bran's reckoning:** a monk's family demands Bran's life as blood-price. Bran can go to his
    death, be exiled, or be defended at the cost of the alliance.
  - **Thrand's proof:** Thrand challenges Asgrim to prove Heid's crimes. Without the evidence,
    he dies in the duel.
  - **The defence of Skarvholm:** whoever leads the watch on the night of the attack may die
    holding the landing, depending on preparation.
  - **Berserk deaths:** a berserker like Kári can die charging alone, through a trait.
  - **The washer at the ford** (finale) names who will die; changing orders can save them, at a
    cost elsewhere.
- **Every death is consequential:** grief (happiness falls household-wide), lost skills and jobs,
  changed relationships, a grave to tend, songs at the fire, ghosts or fetches where the dead had
  unfinished business, story branches that close, and enemies who mock or allies who honour.

## 8. Raids, defences and attacks by land and sea (Books 1, 2, 6, 10)

- **Raids** by sea: stealth or all-out assault (2.2 section 7).
- **Attacks on enemy settlements by land** once on the mainland: march overland with the
  household and allies, and assault a camp, farm or fort, with the same formation and objective
  rules.
- **Sea-borne attacks** on coastal settlements: land in force from the ships, including mainland
  coastal places (Carrick, Kilbrecc, Saltvik's outer harbour, Crowwick, Orm's outposts).
- **Defences** of the homestead and the mainland home: watch, warning, preparation (walls, traps,
  stakes, archers on platforms), then the battle.
- Each is a gate when it is a story event, and optional otherwise.

## 9. Decision log entries
- All traditions shown with their cruelty, even-handedly, within the hard limits.
- Major characters get branching, reactive conversation; minor characters have one or two lines.
- Travel between sites happens off screen with a time cost; sites are explored on foot.
- Points of interest in every site; off-path rewards and hazards.
- Story gates replace fixed chapter dates; the calendar keeps running.
- Dwarf Fortress-style traits, likes, relationships and tendencies.
- Optional quests for ship and character items; up to two companions.
- Downed allies survive if brought home; scripted and choice-driven deaths with consequences.
- Raids, defences and land and sea assaults.
